PRACTICE TABLE · OFFICIAL FAQ DRILLS

Battle drills

Nine short situations to practise the rules that are easy to mis-time or misread when one person is managing several Gearlocs and the shared Battle Mat.

How to use this page: read each situation, decide what happens, then reveal the ruling. These are focused official FAQ clarifications—not a replacement for the applicable rulebook, Gearloc reference sheet, Baddie Skills reference or Encounter card. The official CTG support hub and FAQ index were checked on 25 November 2026; the individual article modification dates are shown by the publisher.

Drill 1 — Choose the order of start-of-turn effects

Situation: Patches has 1 HP and Poison 1. At the start of his turn, he has an innate ability that can heal him. Do you have to take Poison damage before healing?

Ruling: No. Start-of-turn effects are resolved in the order chosen by the player. CTG's example specifically allows Patches to heal before taking Poison damage.

Second example: if a Baddie with Recover 1 is also affected by Poison, you may choose to apply Poison before Recover; CTG notes this can defeat the Baddie before it heals.

Keep separate: Fatigue happens at the beginning of a Round, not at the start of a turn, and affects all Baddies and Gearlocs simultaneously.

Read CTG's start-of-turn clarification ↗

Drill 2 — A unit blocks movement

Situation: a Gearloc's route across the Battle Mat passes through a space occupied by another unit. Can the Gearloc move through that unit?

Ruling: No. Gearlocs and Baddies cannot move through other units; units restrict movement on the Battle Mat.

Tactical lesson: occupied spaces matter when planning routes. CTG also notes that positioning can sometimes use other Baddies to block a Gearloc or a troublesome Melee Baddie. Treat that as a tactical possibility, not a guarantee that every attack is blocked.

Read CTG's movement clarification ↗

Drill 3 — No valid target, no attack for Bones

Situation: a Gearloc has Attack dice available but no legal target. Can the Gearloc roll those dice anyway, hoping to roll Bones for the Backup Plan?

Ruling: No. Attacking always requires a valid target unless a card or Skill specifically states otherwise.

Table habit: check target legality before picking up Attack dice. Do not use an attack roll as a free way to generate Bones when there is no valid target.

Read CTG's valid-target clarification ↗

Drill 4 — Count a Baddie's Active Defence correctly

Situation: a Baddie has Defence 2 and already has one Active Defence die on it. How many Defence dice does it roll when it rolls Defence on its turn?

Ruling: Baddies do not reroll their Defence dice. When rolling Defence, subtract the number of Active Defence dice already on the Baddie from its Defence stat. In this example, it rolls one Defence die.

Table habit: count the Defence dice already sitting on the Baddie before rolling; do not simply roll its full printed Defence stat again.

Read CTG's Active Defence clarification ↗

Drill 5 — Check whether the battle has already ended

Situation A: the last ordinary Baddie is defeated. Do you finish the remaining planned turns?

Ruling: No. The battle ends immediately when the last Baddie is defeated. It also ends immediately if the last Gearloc is KO'd. If an Encounter states a different success or failure condition, that condition governs. In a Tyrant battle, defeating the Tyrant ends the battle even if Baddies remain.

Situation B: at the beginning of a Round, Fatigue removes the last HP from the remaining Gearlocs and Baddies at the same time. Is that a win because the Baddies also fell?

Ruling: No. CTG says this is a battle failure: a Gearloc must remain after all Baddies are defeated for success.

Drill 6 — Resolve Carcass's Meat Hooks against Undead

Situation: Carcass deals the defeating blow to an Undead Baddie. Both Meat Hooks and Undead trigger when that unit is defeated. Must Undead resolve first and bring the Baddie back?

Ruling: No. CTG confirms that Meat Hooks and Undead happen at the same time, so you choose their order. If Carcass dealt the defeating blow, resolve Meat Hooks first and put the Undead Baddie on Meat Hooks; it then does not return a second time.

Important limit: Carcass must deal the defeating blow for Meat Hooks to trigger. If another Gearloc defeats the Undead Baddie, Carcass's Meat Hooks does not activate.

Multi-handed lesson: when another Gearloc is about to finish the target, check whether Carcass needs to land the defeating blow. The timing choice only helps if Carcass's own ability has triggered.

Read CTG's Meat Hooks & Undead clarification ↗

Drill 7 — A Bone no longer belongs to its original die

Situation: you roll Bones on Attack dice and place those Bones in your Backup Plan. When you later count how many Attack or Defence dice you can roll, do you subtract the dice that produced those Bones?

Ruling: No. CTG says that once a Bone is applied to the Backup Plan, it no longer has any association with the type of die it came from. It is simply a Bone.

Multi-handed lesson: track the Bones in the Backup Plan as its own resource. Do not keep the original Attack/Defence die type attached to a Bone after it has been placed there.

Read CTG's Backup Plan dice clarification ↗

Drill 8 — Buff HP does not solve every kind of damage

Situation: a Gearloc has Buff HP. Can you use it to protect against True Damage, or count it when a Baddie pursues the strongest or weakest Gearloc?

Ruling: No. CTG confirms that Buff HP does not protect from True Damage and has no impact when Baddies pursue the strongest or weakest Gearloc.

Table habit: keep Buff HP separate from the Gearloc's actual HP when resolving True Damage and strongest/weakest targeting decisions.

Read CTG's Buff HP clarification ↗

Drill 9 — What qualifies as a Hit?

Situation: a Baddie ability or Skill triggers when the Baddie is Hit. An Attack roll is made, but it deals no damage. Did the Baddie take a Hit?

Ruling: No. CTG defines a Hit as a successful Attack: the Attack dice must deal at least 1 damage.

Table habit: check the damage result before resolving anything that triggers on a Hit. A zero-damage Attack is not a Hit under this clarification.

Read CTG's Hit clarification ↗

A simple solo / multi-handed review loop

This is a learning aid, not an extra game phase. Use it to slow down just enough to catch the nine rulings above.

  1. Before moving: check whether another unit blocks the route.
  2. Before attacking: identify a valid target, unless a card or Skill gives an exception.
  3. At the start of a turn: resolve start-of-turn effects in the order you choose.
  4. At the start of a Round: keep Round-start effects such as Fatigue separate from turn-start effects.
  5. When a Baddie rolls Defence: account for its Active Defence dice already on it.
  6. After a decisive result: check the Encounter's success/failure condition immediately; do not carry on after battle end.
  7. When a unit is defeated: check for abilities that trigger at defeat and whether the official ruling lets you choose their order. For Carcass, Meat Hooks only triggers when Carcass dealt the defeating blow.
  8. When you place a Bone in the Backup Plan: treat it as a Bone, not as an Attack or Defence die.
  9. When resolving True Damage or strongest/weakest targeting: do not use Buff HP as protection or as part of the targeting comparison.
  10. When a rule says Hit: confirm the Attack dealt at least 1 damage before triggering Hit-based abilities.
Scope boundary: these drills do not establish a campaign setup, Encounter-card count, Drellen sequence, Tyrant-specific setup or universal turn order. Those must still be taken from the current official rulebook, the selected Tyrant and the relevant Encounter instructions. The academy will leave an item unresolved rather than infer it.
