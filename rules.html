Rules reference

A practical quick reference built from Chip Theory Games' own rulebooks, reference sheets, FAQs and errata. Every rule clarification below links to the publisher's support article.

Authority rule: Chip Theory Games is the only rules authority used by this academy. This page summarises selected official FAQ clarifications; it is not a complete rulebook. Always check the rulebook for your game/expansion and the relevant card or Gearloc reference sheet first. The publisher says its Too Many Bones FAQ is kept updated with rulings, clarifications and errata. Open the official support hub ↗.

Battle rulings verified against official FAQs

Checked 18 November 2026. These are publisher clarifications, not house rules.

1. Start-of-turn effects

Resolve start-of-turn effects in the order chosen by the player. The official example allows Patches to heal before Poison damage is applied. Fatigue is different: it occurs at the beginning of a Round, not a turn, and affects all Baddies and Gearlocs simultaneously.

Read CTG's start-of-turn clarification ↗

2. Active Defence on Baddies

Baddies do not reroll their Active Defence dice on their turn. When rolling Defence, subtract the number of Active Defence dice already on the Baddie from its Defence stat. CTG's example: a Baddie with Defence 2 and one Active Defence die rolls one Defence die.

Read CTG's Active Defence clarification ↗

3. Movement through units

Gearlocs and Baddies cannot move through other units on the Battle Mat. Other units restrict movement, so positioning can be used to block routes and protect a Gearloc from a melee attacker.

Read CTG's movement clarification ↗

4. Attacking without a valid target

An attack requires a valid target unless a card or Skill specifically says otherwise. Do not roll Attack dice solely to try to generate Bones when no valid target exists.

Read CTG's valid-target clarification ↗

5. When a battle ends

A battle ends immediately when the last Baddie is defeated, or when the last Gearloc is KO'd. If an Encounter states a different success or failure condition, the battle ends as soon as that condition is met. In a Tyrant battle, defeating the Tyrant ends the battle even if Baddies remain.

Read CTG's battle-end clarification ↗

6. Fatigue defeats both sides at once

If Fatigue removes the last HP from the remaining Gearlocs and Baddies simultaneously, the battle is a failure. A Gearloc must remain after all Baddies are defeated for the battle to count as a success.

Read CTG's simultaneous-Fatigue clarification ↗

7. Effect dice when a unit leaves the mat

Effect dice can only remain on units that are on the Battle Mat. When a unit is removed from the mat, remove any Effect dice on it.

Read CTG's Effect-dice clarification ↗

8. Patches' healing range

Patches does not need to be adjacent to another Gearloc to heal them. This is a Patches-specific clarification; it does not change other targeting or range rules.

Read CTG's Patches clarification ↗

9. Party size when playing multi-handed

CTG defines party size by Gearlocs, not by the number of physical units on the Battle Mat: only Gearlocs count; KO'd Gearlocs still count; Lab Rats count as one Gearloc; companions and temporary allies do not count. CTG says party size is normally synonymous with player count, except when a player controls multiple Gearlocs (“multi-handed”).

Practical distinction: record both the number of human players and the number of Gearlocs. If one person controls two Gearlocs, do not automatically substitute “1 player” wherever a rule or card asks for party size. Check the specific setup instruction and use the Gearloc count where it asks for party size.

Read CTG's party-size clarification ↗

10. Buff HP belongs only to Gearlocs

Only Gearlocs can have Buff HP. It is kept in the Prep Area, and other units (including Baddies and Allies) do not have Prep Areas. If an ability would grant Buff HP to a Baddie, Ally or another non-Gearloc, ignore that part of the ability.

Read CTG's Buff HP clarification ↗

Baddie skills and effects: official clarifications

Checked 18 November 2026 against CTG's official Baddie Skills and Encounter Terms FAQ category. These clarifications resolve common edge cases; the Baddie Skills reference and the individual Baddie remain the primary instructions.

11. Poison is not cumulative

A unit does not add multiple Poison values together. When a new Poison Effect is applied, set the Poison value to the more recently applied value, even if that value is lower. For example, applying Poison 1 after Poison 2 changes the unit's Poison value to 1; applying Poison 2 after Poison 1 changes it to 2.

Read CTG's Poison clarification ↗

12. A Baddie with Flee

When a Baddie flees, remove it from the Battle Mat and place it in the defeated Baddie stack. The closest opposing unit with Loot must discard a Loot. A Baddie with both Flee and Treasure does not drop a Loot die when it flees; you must defeat it yourself to receive that Loot die.

If its Flee text refers to Round 5 but it flees in Round 2, it is already gone. The Round 5 instruction matters if the Baddie has remained in the Battle Queue and does not enter the Battle Mat until Round 3 or 4.

Read CTG's Flee clarification ↗

13. Signal adds one Baddie at a time

Signal 2, Signal 3 and similar values do not mean adding that many Baddies at once. The value is the number of Rounds in which the Baddie will add a single Baddie to the Battle Queue on its turn. CTG's example: a 5-point Baddie with Signal 3 adds one 1-point Baddie to the Battle Queue on its turn for three Rounds.

Read CTG's Signal clarification ↗

14. Thick Skin and Defence dice

When resolving an attack against Thick Skin, remove Defence dice first. Then reduce the total incoming damage by the number shown on Thick Skin; if no number is shown, use 1. CTG specifically notes that True Damage effects such as Poison or Bleed can help mitigate Thick Skin.

Read CTG's Thick Skin clarification ↗

15. Engulf damaging a Baddie with Break

If a Baddie with Engulf damages another Baddie that has Break, the Engulf Baddie's Attack dice are exhausted. CTG confirms that Break exhausts Baddie Attack dice in applicable situations.

Read CTG's Engulf and Break clarification ↗

Specific Encounter walkthrough: Gearlocs Need Food Badly

Official CTG Encounter clarification, modified 15 September 2026. This is a special procedure for this Encounter, not the normal battle sequence.

Why this matters: this Encounter changes the battle's activation pattern and restricts what Gearlocs can do. Read the Encounter card, then use the following publisher clarification to resolve its unusual critter movement and Gearloc turns.

Set up the Battle Mat

  1. Place the Gearlocs in their normal starting positions according to their attack form.
  2. Put one HP chip in every Battle Mat space that does not contain a Gearloc. These HP chips represent the critters and form the stacks that can grow during the Encounter.

Resolve each critter turn

  1. Use the rolled lane: all critters in that lane activate.
  2. Those critters move simultaneously, each moving on top of an adjacent existing critter in a different lane. Add the moving critter's HP chip to the stack it joins.
  3. If a moving critter has no adjacent critter to move onto, remove that critter instead.
  4. Resolve the lane's movement simultaneously, not as a chain of one-at-a-time moves. CTG explains that this prevents critters from stacking onto adjacent critters in the same lane. For example, a critter in Lane 1 moves to an adjacent space in Lane 2 or Lane 3.

Alternate critters and Gearlocs

  1. After the critters activate, choose one Gearloc to activate.
  2. Activate the critters again, then choose a second Gearloc.
  3. Continue alternating critter activations and Gearloc activations. Gearlocs may be chosen in any order, but every Gearloc must take one turn before any Gearloc takes a second turn.

Two-Gearloc illustration: Critters → Gearloc A → Critters → Gearloc B → Critters → Gearloc A. This illustrates the alternating order only; the player may choose which Gearloc goes first.

Gearloc actions and the HP stacks

  • Gearlocs use Dex to move and Atk to attack critters, using their normal attack form.
  • Gearlocs cannot move through critters.
  • Skills and Backup Plans cannot be used in this Encounter.
  • To defeat a critter stack and take that HP stack as Buff HP, deal damage equal to the number of HP chips in the stack.
  • If the attack deals less damage than the stack contains HP chips, the stack is removed but no Buff HP is gained.
Keep this rule local to this Encounter. Do not apply its alternating critter/Gearloc activation pattern or its Skills and Backup Plans restriction to ordinary battles. Use this procedure only when resolving Gearlocs Need Food Badly.

Read CTG's complete official Encounter clarification ↗

How to verify a rules question

  1. Identify the exact game, expansion, Gearloc, Tyrant, card and timing window involved.
  2. Check the relevant official rulebook and reference sheet from the CTG Too Many Bones support hub.
  3. Search the official Too Many Bones FAQ index for a clarification or erratum.
  4. Record the conclusion with the exact official source link and the date checked.
  5. If no official source resolves the question, mark it unresolved and ask CTG support. Do not fill the gap with a fan interpretation.
