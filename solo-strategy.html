TACTICAL THINKING · PATH 06

Solo strategy lab

Learn to run a party of Gearlocs by yourself without confusing player count, party size, individual character state or the shared battle.

The core solo questions

True solo or multiple Gearlocs?

Compare cognitive load, action economy, scaling and character synergy. Keep the human-player count separate from the Gearloc party size.

What should I develop?

Balance stats, skill access, reliable dice, survival and the specific threat profile of the chosen Tyrant. Treat build advice as strategy, not rules.

When should I take a risk?

Consider current health, remaining days, reward value, failure costs and whether a later encounter becomes harder.

How do I use the Battle Mat?

Prioritise positioning, initiative, range, target access and preventing dangerous enemies from taking effective turns.

One player does not always mean a one-Gearloc party

Official CTG clarification: party size counts Gearlocs, not human players. KO'd Gearlocs still count; Lab Rats count as one Gearloc; companions and temporary allies do not count. Party size normally matches player count except when a player controls multiple Gearlocs.

Example: one human controls Picket and Patches. Human players = 1; Gearlocs = 2; party size = 2. If Picket is KO'd, the party size remains 2. Adding a companion or temporary ally does not increase it.

Read the official CTG party-size clarification ↗

When an instruction explicitly asks for party size, do not replace it with the number of humans at the table. If an instruction asks for players, preserve that wording and follow the selected game's official rulebook. This distinction does not create a new setup recipe: Encounter selection, card counts and Tyrant setup still come from the applicable rulebook and Tyrant instructions.

A practical multi-handed table procedure

This is an academy organisation and decision aid, not an alternative rules sequence. The rulebook, Encounter card, Gearloc reference sheet and official FAQ always take precedence.

Suggested first multi-handed learning load: use two Gearlocs, rather than trying to manage a larger party on the first attempt. Picket and Patches are the academy's provisional teaching pair. This is a learning recommendation, not a CTG rule or a claim that they are the strongest combination.

Before the battle

  1. Write the two counts down. Record human players and Gearlocs separately. For one person controlling Picket and Patches, write 1 human player / 2 Gearlocs / party size 2.
  2. Give each Gearloc a clear home. Keep each character's mat, chip, personal dice, HP and character-specific state together. Do not let one Gearloc's dice or status markers drift into the other Gearloc's area.
  3. Keep shared battle state central. Leave the Battle Mat, Encounter card, Baddie queue, round tracking and other shared components in one common area.
  4. Read the Encounter before placing anything. Note its special setup, success/failure condition and any exception to the normal battle procedure. Then follow the selected rulebook and card exactly.
  5. Check each Gearloc separately. Use that Gearloc's official reference sheet for its skills, dice, costs and timing. Do not assume an ability works the same way for both characters.

During the battle

  1. Follow the official activation procedure. Use the initiative, round and turn sequence in the applicable rulebook and any special instructions on the Encounter. Do not invent a combined activation just because one person controls the party.
  2. Make the current Gearloc's decisions. When the rules call for that Gearloc to act, consider only the actions and resources available to that Gearloc, while taking the other Gearloc's position and support options into account.
  3. Re-check the board after every activation. Reassess legal targets, movement routes, threats, status effects and whether the Encounter's end condition has been met.
  4. Track each character independently. Check HP, dice availability, effects and character-specific resources on the correct mat or area. Keep round-level effects separate from turn-level effects; see the official battle clarifications.
  5. Stop when the official end condition is met. CTG confirms that a battle ends as soon as its success/failure condition is met. Do not continue resolving turns after that point.

Worked example: Picket and Patches

Situation: one person is controlling Picket and Patches in the same party.

  • Record party size as 2, not 1, because there are two Gearlocs.
  • Keep Picket's and Patches' personal components and character state distinct.
  • When Patches uses a healing ability, CTG's FAQ confirms that Patches does not need to be adjacent to the Gearloc being healed. The ability's own costs, target restrictions and other conditions still apply; check Patches' official reference sheet.
  • If either Gearloc is KO'd, continue to count that Gearloc in party size for any relevant party-size instruction.

Solo controller's decision pause

Before committing an activation, ask these questions. They are strategic prompts, not additional game rules.

  1. Which enemy or effect is the biggest immediate threat?
  2. Can this Gearloc deal with it using a legal action now?
  3. Would the other Gearloc be better placed to solve it on its own activation?
  4. What resource, position or future option will this choice spend?
  5. What changes if the next enemy activation or the next Round happens first?
Important boundary: this procedure does not specify the Encounter deck, day count, Special Encounter selection, Tyrant Encounter placement or Baddie count. Those details vary with the selected game and Tyrant and must be taken from their current official instructions. The withdrawn Drellen card recipe remains withdrawn.

General decision framework

  1. State the goal: what outcome must this turn or Encounter achieve?
  2. Identify constraints: legal actions, available dice, position, health and special rules.
  3. Compare choices: expected benefit, failure risk and future cost.
  4. Check interactions: initiative, statuses, targeting and ability timing.
  5. Choose and review: after resolution, note whether the assumptions held.
