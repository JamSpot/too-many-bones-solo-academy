Baddie analysis framework

For each enemy family and relevant individual Baddie, the guide will record its role, movement, range, attack profile, keywords, special ability and the tactical problem it creates. The aim is to explain what the enemy changes about your turn—not simply restate its stats.

Battle planning checklist

  • Read all special rules before placing the first Baddie.
  • Identify enemies that can create immediate defeat risks.
  • Check initiative and likely order of actions.
  • Estimate whether a target can be defeated before it acts.
  • Account for movement, range, obstacles and target restrictions.
  • Track ongoing effects and trigger timing precisely.
  • Reassess priorities whenever a new Baddie enters or the board state changes.
Important: matchup advice depends on Gearloc, party size, difficulty and the particular edition or expansion rules. Dossiers will state their assumptions instead of presenting one universal answer.

Official Tyrant FAQ rulings

Checked against CTG's current official Too Many Bones FAQ index and Tyrants category on 21 November 2026. These individual clarifications are older publisher rulings; the date here is the date they were rechecked, not their original publication date.

These answers resolve specific timing and ability questions. They do not replace the chosen Tyrant's card, the relevant Encounter card, or the setup instructions in the correct official rulebook. Apply each clarification only to the named Tyrant or battle.

Marrow: one Backup Plan per turn

If Marrow rolls 3 Bones, he still cannot use both his 1-Bone and 2-Bone Backup Plans on the same turn. CTG's answer is one 5-point Orc from his 2-Bone Backup Plan. Baddies and Tyrants, like Gearlocs, may use their Backup Plan only once per turn.

Read CTG's Marrow clarification ↗

Mulmesh: choose the order of the threshold check and start-of-turn damage

Mulmesh's check for having 3 HP or less remaining happens at the beginning of his turn. Poison and Bleed also resolve at the beginning of his turn. CTG says this is a tie in timing, so the player chooses the order.

Practical use: if Poison or Bleed damage would bring Mulmesh to 3 HP or less, you may resolve that damage before the check. You may instead check first. Choose the order deliberately and apply the same timing consistently.

Read CTG's Mulmesh timing clarification ↗

Nom: a revived Gearloc does not automatically return to the mat

During Nom's battle, a revived Gearloc regains the HP specified by the reviving Skill, but does not reappear on the Battle Mat at the end of that Round. The Encounter rules allow only one Gearloc in play at a time. The revived Gearloc can return after another Gearloc is KO'd.

Read CTG's Nom clarification ↗

Goblin Queen's Guard: movement after it is displaced

If a Skill or Loot moves the Guard away from its A/B spots, each time it acts it moves one space towards the B Spot. Once it reaches B, it continues moving between B and A.

Read CTG's Goblin Queen's Guard clarification ↗

Kollossum: Synapse when the destination is blocked

If the closest fellow Gearloc has no available adjacent spaces, the Weakest Gearloc does not move. The Weakest Gearloc still rolls Attack dice against that fellow Gearloc if it can perform a Ranged Attack.

Read CTG's Kollossum Synapse clarification ↗

Rol: move to a Rock space beside the strongest opponent

Rol identifies the strongest opposing unit by current HP. If a Rock space is adjacent to that unit, Rol moves the number of spaces needed to reach the eligible Rock space. If there are multiple strongest opponents or multiple eligible Rock spaces, she chooses the Rock space requiring the least movement. This movement can exceed two spaces.

Rol can move even when already adjacent to a Gearloc if that Gearloc is not the strongest opposing unit, or if Rol is not already on a Rock space. She does not move only when no reachable Rock space is adjacent to the strongest opponent, or when she is already on Rock and adjacent to that strongest opponent.

Rol considers Rok an opposing unit when identifying the strongest opponent and can attack Rok. She does not consider other Baddies opposing units, so she will not attack them; those enemies may still treat Rol as an opponent and attack her.
